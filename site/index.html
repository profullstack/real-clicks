A 2003-style traffic broker would sell all 579k. In 2026 most of that is software, and reselling it is ad fraud, whatever you call it. So our board voted against a traffic exchange and for the opposite: sell only the people who ask for something, and prove they are real.

How the pilot works

One quote form, one vendor, one price.

  1. A "get a quote" form sits on the GPU and server pages of r4ck.dev and on the pricing and features pages of threatcrush.com. (Not on c0mpute.com: it sells GPU time itself.)
  2. Each request is email-verified and scored against ThreatCrush reputation data. The visitor has to be an Active user: real typing and pointer use on the form, at least 5 seconds on it, an empty honeypot, and a browser that doesn't report automation.
  3. The consent line names the one vendor who gets the lead. We never resell it or share it with a list.
  4. Vendors prepay a balance with CoinPay (crypto or PayPal): $50 per verified lead, flat. The first 2 leads are free samples. No contract, stop anytime. If a lead is invalid, one click puts the $50 back.
  5. At most 5 vendors. Leads route by email automatically, no human in the loop.
  6. Day 14 (9 Dec): if there are fewer than 10 qualified requests, fewer than 2 paying or committed vendors, or more than about 3 founder hours a week on vendors, we kill it, ship affiliate links plus marketing instead, and say so here.

Together those pages were 98% crawlers. ThreatCrush has real people but far too few to produce 10 quote requests in 14 days. At first no quote forms went up and no vendor was offered a lead. Then the owner restarted the pilot: the forms and vendor offers went out on 25 Nov, and day 14 decides. Option A, a traffic exchange, is never on the table.

Verdict, 3 to 2: run a 14-day lead test, not a traffic exchange. All five AI advisors rejected buying and selling raw traffic. The CFO, Growth Marketer and Contrarian Investor backed a vertical lead pilot with affiliate links alongside it. The Operator and Founder-Time Strategist wanted affiliate links first. The Contrarian was the only one to change their mind, moving from links to leads.

How do I use this?

real.clicks lives on Moshpit, a namespace beside the old one where anyone can own an ending like .clicks. The pit.world copy, real.clicks.pit.world, works in any browser with nothing to install. To open https://real.clicks directly, about two minutes:

  1. Turn on secure DNS in your browser (Chrome, Edge, Brave: Settings, Privacy and security, Use secure DNS, Custom. Firefox: Settings, Privacy & Security, DNS over HTTPS) and enter https://dns.moshcode.sh/dns-query.
  2. Install the Moshpit certificate so the padlock shows: moshpit-root-ca.crt.
  3. Open https://real.clicks.

Every device, and how to undo it: pit.world/connect. Or install moshcode: curl -fsSL https://moshcode.sh/install.sh | sh

real.clicks also has a board and an AT Protocol social feed. One pit.world account signs you in on both, and on every other name's.
